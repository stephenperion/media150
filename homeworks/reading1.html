Reading 1 - "In Plato's Cave" By Susan Sontag

Reading “In Plato’s Cave” by Susan Sontag, I was propelled into the question, “what is Plato’s Cave”. Throughout the text I was looking for a specific definition/concrete meaning given by the author and how they interpret “Plato’s Cave”. In her text, originally from “On Photography”, she goes over photographs and how they collect the world. Sontag gives a comparison between the difference of photographs with other forms of media. She mentions movies and television programs and how they light up walls, flicker, and then go out. In contrast, photographs are much more convenient form of media due to it being an object, lightweight, cheap to produce, easy to carry, accumulate, and store. In summary, photography is an object of personal value. Unlike movies and/or television programs, which are stuck to antennas and/or radio/satellite communications, photography and photographs can stick beside a person for the ability carry, hold on to, and to further cherish due to the person permanently having them. Sontag carries this narrative of personal belonging for photographs through the idea of preservation and cherishing the contents within the photograph. Through the process of photography and capturing the contents of what was taken for the photograph, it provides a sense of longevity and/or immortality. It captures the contents of what was taken. Photographs capture data, statistics, and concrete evidence in visual for of what was. It also captures people, physical objects/relics, and landscapes of what was. Moreover, photographs capture the moments, memories, and the feeling of what was. Through the phrase “what was”, it relates to Sontag’s primary statement that photographs are objects. Just like any object, it deteriorates, erodes, and decay into the past. Fading away.

Though fading away, people still continue to capture the moments that feels precious to them—a sense of keeping and furnishing of what they experienced to carry it to the future. To some, Sontag mentions, as a form of art in which they further capture things of nature and abstract concepts to keep them preserved. Just like any form of art, photography too can be idealized as a form of “what is needed”. Sontag mentions images are being idealized, and with no further act of cherishment to the actual contents of the photo, it becomes plain and lacks the point of preservation. Instead of trying to capture photographs that portray painting-like images fully capturing the feeling and ambiance of the contents of the subject, people stray away from this artistic perspective to capture subjects that don’t fully respect the original standing and foundations of taking photographs. From capturing feelings and the senses of the subject, fully resonating with it/them, they have turned away to a form of idealizing about the subject. Throughout the text, I was looking for the author’s meaning about “Plato’s Cave” and how they see this term as in their lenses. The dictionary defines “Plato’s Cave” as “a metaphor illustrating the journey from ignorance to enlightenment”, and I think Susan Sontag captures this perfectly. She is trying to define photography as a form of relationship from audience to subject—resonating with the photograph’s subject. Though, due to idealistic ideas, people fully stray away from having this relationship, and think of it as a foreign and untouchable entity—ignorance to enlightenment.
