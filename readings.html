Reading 1 - "In Plato's Cave" By Susan Sontag


Reading “In Plato’s Cave” by Susan Sontag, I was propelled into the question, “what is Plato’s Cave”. Throughout the text I was looking for a specific definition/concrete meaning given by the author and how they interpret “Plato’s Cave”. In her text, originally from “On Photography”, she goes over photographs and how they collect the world. Sontag gives a comparison between the difference of photographs with other forms of media. She mentions movies and television programs and how they light up walls, flicker, and then go out. In contrast, photographs are much more convenient form of media due to it being an object, lightweight, cheap to produce, easy to carry, accumulate, and store. In summary, photography is an object of personal value. Unlike movies and/or television programs, which are stuck to antennas and/or radio/satellite communications, photography and photographs can stick beside a person for the ability carry, hold on to, and to further cherish due to the person permanently having them. Sontag carries this narrative of personal belonging for photographs through the idea of preservation and cherishing the contents within the photograph. Through the process of photography and capturing the contents of what was taken for the photograph, it provides a sense of longevity and/or immortality. It captures the contents of what was taken. Photographs capture data, statistics, and concrete evidence in visual for of what was. It also captures people, physical objects/relics, and landscapes of what was. Moreover, photographs capture the moments, memories, and the feeling of what was. Through the phrase “what was”, it relates to Sontag’s primary statement that photographs are objects. Just like any object, it deteriorates, erodes, and decay into the past. Fading away.

Though fading away, people still continue to capture the moments that feels precious to them—a sense of keeping and furnishing of what they experienced to carry it to the future. To some, Sontag mentions, as a form of art in which they further capture things of nature and abstract concepts to keep them preserved. Just like any form of art, photography too can be idealized as a form of “what is needed”. Sontag mentions images are being idealized, and with no further act of cherishment to the actual contents of the photo, it becomes plain and lacks the point of preservation. Instead of trying to capture photographs that portray painting-like images fully capturing the feeling and ambiance of the contents of the subject, people stray away from this artistic perspective to capture subjects that don’t fully respect the original standing and foundations of taking photographs. From capturing feelings and the senses of the subject, fully resonating with it/them, they have turned away to a form of idealizing about the subject. Throughout the text, I was looking for the author’s meaning about “Plato’s Cave” and how they see this term as in their lenses. The dictionary defines “Plato’s Cave” as “a metaphor illustrating the journey from ignorance to enlightenment”, and I think Susan Sontag captures this perfectly. She is trying to define photography as a form of relationship from audience to subject—resonating with the photograph’s subject. Though, due to idealistic ideas, people fully stray away from having this relationship, and think of it as a foreign and untouchable entity—ignorance to enlightenment.

Reading 2 - "In Plato's Cave" By Susan Sontag


The discussion concerning Artificial Intelligence has become even more worrisome. Though some people use Artificial Intelligence as a form of entertainment, some lighthearted and unfunny-funny content, some take it as a form of extreme content making—deepfakes, false narratives, and much more. The supposed vision of Artificial Intelligence was supposed to make the lives of humans even more convenient and elevate the human race in a futuristic time-period, just like the movies. Though, these uses of Artificial Intelligence, deepfakes, lack of human interaction, and much more, has proven to degrade the reality of which humans live in today, according to Nesrine Malik in her article “With ‘AI slop’ distorting our reality, the world is sleepwalking into disaster”.

One of the ways Artificial Intelligence degrades the reality of which humans live today is through its function of generative images. These generative images provides fake images, and sometimes even videos, to create a story by the person generating their prompt into Artificial Intelligence. These fake photos and videos are then used to create false narratives to “benefit” the prompt writer’s intentions. This is seen in Malik’s article through her statement, “There are entire YouTube videos of made-up scenarios in which Trump prevail against liberal forces”, all entirely made out of the generative images that Artificial Intelligence produces. Though just one example out of a multitude amount of content on the internet, it just proves the people’s ability to create “realistic” narratives through the fake images and videos that they post to convey their own intention-filled narratives.

Another way that Artificial Intelligence further degrades the reality that humans live in today is creating content that was not the original purpose of Artificial Intelligence. One prime example of this is the use of Deepfakes. Deepfakes are synthetic—or man-made/fake—images, audio, or videos created by Artificial Intelligence to fabricate and/or alter a person’s likeness. Commonly, deepfakes are used to create a false narrative based on people in real life to make them do something that they have never done, say, or acted upon. An entirely fake narrative that ruins the original person’s identity. People have generated pornographic content, false identities, and even create a “real” connection towards these deepfakes. There have been several cases in which these deepfakes has ruined the lives of regular people, politicians, and especially celebrities. Malik states this use of deepfakes always “leads to the desensitization and overwhelming of visual palates. The overall effect of being exposed to AI images all the time, from the nonsensical to the soothing to the ideological, is that everything begins to land in a different way”.

These uses of Artificial Intelligence have ruined people’s social relations, reputations, and much more. The origins of Artificial Intelligence and to generate a better future for man-kind, has not proven itself through these false uses of Artificial Intelligence. Ruining the original abilities of mankind to be creative, social, and to respect each other.

Reading 3 - "Graphic Novels: Motherhood in crisis" By Publisher Aljazeera


Opening the website to do this reading, I expected to read an article. Though as I clicked onto the stories that were presented by the article, I was immediately shocked that it was a graphic novel. In an academic setting, graphic novels are not commonly used to build an assignment. Though, with the use of graphic novels in the article it helps build a visual illustration of the people, pain, and overall story that is told.

This article/graphic novel by Aljazeera, titled “Graphic Novels: Motherhood in crisis”, goes into the themes of the pain and struggles of childbirth and other stories that may have affected these women. Primarily based in several locations in Africa, these graphic novels go into telling the stories of women that has struggled with womanhood in terms of community, pain, and the inability of society to support them as pregnant mothers medically.

One of the themes that was presented in the graphic novels is community and womanhood. Often, in these graphic novels are shown to struggle with the darker themes of rape, abuse, and pain of being affected by the things that are put onto them. In a community-based setting, these women are ostracized and stranded by themselves without any rock of protection from the hurtful whispers, assumptions, and accusations put on them. Moreover, the stories told in the graphic novels, these women are the victims—but the community says otherwise. Though, in contrast to this, due to having these sorts of pain, similar women commonly confide in each other—further pushing each other that things will get better, and that pain is only temporary. Primarily seen in the first graphic novel, the main character found solace in her two new friends, that was able to lift her up, even when her own community shunned her for her situation.

A second theme that I saw in these graphic novels was the inability of society and the government to support these, commonly, young women. With such deep struggles and trauma, people would commonly confide in their safety places, seen in people, mentors, and even places. Though, these women who had their stories told via graphic novels, needed the support in terms of emotional and physical. Thanks to programs given by the school and social programs, these women were able to emotionally confide in therapists to release and process what they had gone through properly. Though emotional programs were present, there was an absence of physical support. A commonality in the stories told, there was a struggle in medical support given by the African government. There were diseases and an overall insufficient amount of resources to support these women medically. And with such insufficiency, it just shows the need for these programs.

With these graphic novels and articles making time to tell these stories, it makes way for the voices of these women who struggled through these to have a platform and tell other women that they are not alone.

Reading 4 - "Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It" By Publisher The New York Times


An article by The New York Times, titled “Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It,” goes over the fears, information, and the overall effects that nuclear bombs have when it is used. Though the highlight of this article is the way it is told and portrayed.

The main highlight for me about this article is the way it is portrayed through its format. Upon opening the website, the audience is greeted with a black screen and an alarming hint of red blurring on and off with the black background. With the first few words opening up as “The risk of nuclear conflict is rising.” With a short introduction and the visual presentation given in this article, it shows that this is a special article—an important article with a message to tell, and further emphasized by being posted with such a platform as The New York Times. As the viewer continues to scroll, it continues with the same black and alarming red background, further showing words that the article wants to depict to its audience. A narrative that The New York Times wants to say to its audience.

While the black and alarming red background builds a narrative to tell to their audience, it suddenly goes white into a regular article. In other words, The New York Times uses the black and alarming red background to convey the words they want their audiences to hear and a regular article format to convey the standard information that is needed to build their narrative.

Another creative choice The New York Times made was to make illustrations visually depicting nuclear warfare. These visual illustrations tell the story of a nuclear bomb exploding at its destination, the fire, and the destruction that comes as a result of this bomb. Though one of the main pieces, The New York Times has produced among these illustrations is the smoke and aftermath of a nuclear bomb. Showing and depicting the pain and anxiety that looms onto the nation and its people.

Anxiety, trauma, and pain clings onto the hearts of the innocent people that is affected by a nuclear bomb. They face the type of pain that looms over them over generations. Besides the emotional stress that people would have to face, the overall radiation left by a nuclear bomb is what affects the health of biolife of the place. Seen in another illustration shown by The New York Times, with descriptive and informative facts given by the publisher. Some of the facts given states, “Days or weeks after exposure (to the radiation), people who look fine can suddenly lose hunks of hair, become anemic and weak, and begin bleeding internally. Their immune systems can fail, rendering them helpless against the infectious diseases that start to spread: dysentery, typhoid, cholera.” Nuclear bombs can be an immediate solution for nations at time of war, but its effects on the people, life, and a nation is everlasting and will loom over them for many years to come.

Extra Credit - "Artist Talk: THE BLACK BOX" By Nancy Baker


Going to the event hosted by the Hunter Media Department for a talk and presentation by Nancy Baker, I was really in shock at the types of work she personally presented. MEDPL 150, as a class, goes over the practical skills and creative knowledge one needs to succeed creatively in the modern day. When Nancy Baker presented about her art, mediums, and professional standing in the creative world, I saw how the foundations I learned at MEDPL 150 are implemented in the professional creative world.

Attending the Artist Talk, presented by Nancy Baker, there were several things I enjoyed as an audience member. One of the main things is the presenter herself. Nancy started off with her presentation with such strong personal pieces that the audience could tell she really loves. With each piece that she showed, it looked like she was beaming with enthusiasm, passion, and love for what she does. I was astonished by her statement that she is mostly a self-taught artist, especially with such creative and innovative pieces of modern and transcendent art. As a self-taught artist for the majority of my life, I was so in shock and happy that someone as innovative as Nancy Baker could climb such heights in the professional world. With each slide she showed, I could tell it was purposely hand-crafted and picked to best tell the story of her art and the reason as to why she pursues what she pursues.

Moving on to her art pieces, some brief words I would say to describe her art are innovative, modern, creative, and transcendent. The reason for this is that she mainly focuses on transcendent art—art that, defined by Google, is “creative work that goes beyond physical reality, aiming to evoke profound emotional, spiritual, or intellectual experiences that connect the viewer to something beyond themselves.” With her personal medium of using VR—virtual reality—art, she can create pieces of art that are inventive and groundbreaking with the use of technology. One of the pieces that I distinctly remember is her art in New York City. This piece, from a simple glance, looks like a flying whale above the skies of New York City. Though upon closer inspection, this whale-like figure looks futuristic, alien, and very out-of-this-world. Nancy’s art is definitely art that is distinct and transcends the boundaries of what art could be.

In relation to what I have been learning in MEDPL 150, I saw the practical skills that I am currently learning and what they could look like in the future when applied—seen, and proven with how Nancy tackles and pursues her creative projects. I saw how the artistic foundations of composition, unity, space, and proximity play a role in how the story of art can be told to a wide array of audiences. Simultaneously, while keeping the foundations of art knowledge intact, how it is being executed through mediums such as Photoshop, Illustrator, and many more mediums has a major impact on how the art pieces are visually shown.

Attending the Artist Talk: THE BLACK BOX has definitely inspired me in many ways. From learning the basics and the practicals to looking at my potential in the far future, with what I can do creatively. I hope I can further put into practice what I know and am currently learning to be such an inventive artist as Nancy Baker.
